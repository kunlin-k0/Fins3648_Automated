# Problem definition and market opportunity (CEO)

Read `/CLAUDE.md`, `/STYLE.md`, `/course/00-assessment-spec.md` and the relevant
`/course/week-*.md` before writing. Every claim needs an (a)/(b)/(c) tag.

## Required outputs
1. **Problem statement** — one sentence: *[who] cannot [do what] because [why], costing [what].*
2. **Pain sizing** — how big, for how many, worth how much. Sourced or assumption-tagged.
3. **Why unsolved** — who tried, why they failed or stopped short. If nobody tried, explain why not.
4. **Why now** — the trigger. Prefer regulatory/crisis triggers over "the technology exists".
5. **Competitive map** — named incumbents and challengers, and the gap you occupy.

## Procedure
1. Classify the pain against the W6 categories: **mispricing / inefficient process /
   lack of access / underserved market**. Name which one. If it fits none, the problem is
   probably not sharp enough yet.
2. Size it top-down **and** bottom-up. If the two disagree by more than ~2x, say so rather
   than picking the flattering one.
3. Apply the W1 crisis lens for "why now" — technology availability alone is the weakest
   possible trigger.
4. Locate the gap using the Judo principle: *move to where your opponent isn't; use the
   size of opponents against them* `[W2/case: Judo]`. Name the segment the incumbents
   structurally cannot serve, and say why the constraint is structural.
5. If targeting bank fee income rather than lending, cite the W5 profit-pool point.

## Refuse to produce
- A market size with no derivation.
- "The market is growing rapidly" without a figure and a source.
- A problem statement that is actually a solution statement in disguise.

## Handover
Write `/handover/CEO.md` and a speaker brief. Flag every `[NUMBER NEEDED]` explicitly —
these are the CEO's to close, not to inherit silently.
