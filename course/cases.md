# Case index — what each case is *for*

Cases are the currency of this course. Never state a concept without anchoring it to one.

## Judo Bank — Everything-as-a-Service (W2 architecture; also W5 lending)
**Context.** Founded 2016 by banking executives frustrated with Australian banking. Reached
a **A$1 billion private valuation within 12 months** of getting its banking licence, without
ever owning or operating any IT systems — orchestrating an ecosystem of external providers
instead, at a fraction of normal cost of ownership. One founder: they lacked the ~A$200m
required to build a bank the normal way, so they found another way.

**The strategic inversion.** While competitors deployed self-service technology to *replace*
frontline human interaction and cut cost, Judo automated the **back office** so frontline
bankers had *more* time for interpersonal contact. Technology as enabler, not replacement.

**Target and strategy.** Australian **SMEs** — significantly underserved because the Big Four
concentrated on household lending and mortgages. Core principles: move to where your
opponent isn't; use the size of opponents against them. Products: business loans, credit
lines, equipment loans, business accounts, term deposits.

**Business objectives enabled by EaaS.** Relationship-oriented technology; banking from
anywhere (SME owners prefer meeting on their own premises; also gave business continuity
through bushfires and COVID); lean automated back-office processes.

**Risks.** Three classes — preventable, external, strategic. Third-party dependency is the
core exposure: if providers fail, the bank is affected. APRA treats cloud platforms at the
extreme end of the risk curve, yet approved this because risks were identified and mitigated
up front. Security and privacy were functional requirements of every component; real-time
logging run as a distinct external service.

**Outcome.** Only one of the four neobanks licensed in 2019 to reach profitability (2021).
The others: Xinja ceased banking Dec 2020; 86 400 acquired by NAB Jan 2021; Volt not profitable.

**Use for:** cloud/EaaS cost argument, CAPEX-to-OPEX, build-vs-partner, underserved segments,
third-party risk, tech-as-enabler-not-replacement.

## DBS Bank — digital transformation (W1/W2 incumbent transformation)
Worst-performing service company in Singapore → Euromoney "World's Best Digital Bank" (2016),
Global Finance best bank in the world (2018). In 2014 the board gave the leadership team
**S$200,000,000** to bet on digital, no questions asked. CEO Piyush Gupta: Strategy 2.0 was
"not about technology but about technology **and people**" — the distinction credited for
success where digital execution fails as often as 84% of the time.
Strategy 1.0 (operate at international banking standards) laid the foundation and eliminated
250 million customer hours of non-value-add time. Strategy 2.0 "Making Banking Joyful" ran on
three initiatives: **become digital to the core; embed ourselves in the customer journey;
create a 26,000-person start-up**.
**Use for:** incumbent transformation, culture as the binding constraint, sequencing.

## Ping An (W3)
Traditional Chinese insurer → global tech-driven financial services giant.
**Use for:** ecosystem strategy, technology as a revenue line, incumbent-led AI adoption.

## Kabbage (W5, extended reading)
AI/data-driven US small-business lending; minutes-to-decision using bank account,
transaction and accounting data. Acquired by American Express; later Chapter 11
(KServicing Wind Down Corp, Oct 2022). Criticised on customer service and PPP handling.
**Use for:** alternative credit scoring, automation trade-offs, and — importantly — the
failure side. Cite both sides.
