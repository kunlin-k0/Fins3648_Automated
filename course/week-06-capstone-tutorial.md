# Week 6 — Capstone Project Tutorial

No new theory. This week is method, and it is the single most useful week for this repo.

## Build order (the convenor's own method)
> Lay out the overall proposal first — pain point, market analysis, technology solution,
> business model, P&L — then **pull elements out of the finished plan to make the pitch**.

Doing the deck first risks a presentation inconsistent with the report. You also need an
overall plan before you can credibly pitch it. **Encoded as CLAUDE.md Rule 5.**

## Report narrative flow (same flow as the pitch, more detail)
pain point → how big the pain point is → how innovative the solution is → why your team is
the right team → business model and revenue streams → resources needed, implementation
plan, and how much money you are asking for.

The report should follow this flow start to finish so it tells a story. The pitch may mix
the order for effect; the report should not.

## Sizing the pain
Pain point categories named in the tutorial: **mispricing**, inefficient processes,
lack of access, underserved market.

## Technology justification (hard requirement)
> "We're leveraging AI and then it does magic, and therefore we're proposing this" —
> **far from being acceptable.**

You must justify the choice the way model selection was justified in the property price
prediction case: why this model type, how you decided, and how you explain the outcome.
**Encoded as CLAUDE.md Rule 3.**

## Financial evidence
A spreadsheet showing unit cost of the technology solution, a P&L embedding revenue minus
cost, a **5-year** trend (3 years often insufficient to reach break-even), the effect of
scale economies, break-even and ROI. Expect early losses then a scalable profit curve.

## Report mechanics
- 25 pages **includes** title page and closing page; **excludes** appendices and references.
- Push prototype diagrams, code and user journeys to appendices to preserve page budget.
- Three main parts: introduction, proposed solution (technical **and** business), conclusion.
- Roughly 5 pages per person, but formatting, structure, language and narrative must feel
  like one document, not several stitched together.
- Citation format free but **consistent**.
- Must be realistic to implement — not futuristic.

## Pitch preparation
Three-bullet rule: **brainstorm → organise → create slides and verbalise**; then present and
practise. Keep to three bullets because audiences remember three things, not ten.
Practise repeatedly as a team. You are asking for money — keep it exciting, show potential.

## Pitch structure
Bottom line up front (quantified impact) → pain and response story → how the response works
(solution) → why you are the best team to deliver it → return to the bottom line.
Do not open with product features; features are boring to people who have not felt the pain.

## Gantt / project plan mechanics (Task 1)
Column B = key milestones. Column C = responsible person. Columns D–N = colour-coded start
and finish weeks. Column P = deliverable for each milestone. Five typical milestones:
1. Team formation, initiation and planning
2. Research, market validation, idea validation
3. Design and development
4. Pitch preparation (deck + report)
5. Deliver, defend, evaluate (peer evaluation)

Milestone 5 starts Week 9, ends Week 10. Don't schedule beyond Week 8 for build work —
Weeks 8–9 are for rehearsal and report generation.
