# Week 2 — Fintech Building Blocks and Enabling Strategy

## Innovation
- Innovation is required to stay digitally competitive and relevant.
- **Disruptive innovation** = making the status quo obsolete and redefining the market
  (not merely improving it).
- **Innovation Ambition Matrix** — used to balance an innovation portfolio across core,
  adjacent and transformational bets. Useful for arguing where your solution sits.

## The three building blocks (core diagram — worth reproducing)
A fintech needs IT infrastructure with data storage and processing, secure connections to
partner systems, and algorithms implementing business logic:

- **CLOUD** — digital infrastructure to host the fintech, store and process its data.
- **DATA** — the range of data types supporting decisioning and personalisation.
- **APIs** — connections to partners that improve the offering.

## Cloud
- **Turns CAPEX into OPEX** — scale without owning or maintaining infrastructure. (This is
  the single most reusable line in the whole course for a capstone cost argument.)
- Enhanced security compliance and high-availability infrastructure.
- Cloud-native architecture: microservices, containerisation, API-centric design.
- Full-stack AI development ecosystem moves you from prototyping to production.
- **IaaS**: vast server farms; private (dedicated) vs public (shared); scalable,
  agile on-demand pricing that copes with volatile demand.
- **Hybrid and multi-cloud** are the common deployment models.

## Data
- Structured/numeric data is roughly **1%** of a typical firm's data; around **80%** is
  unstructured (voice, video, text). The upside is in making sense of the unstructured mass.
- Data architecture essentials: pipelines, storage, privacy, governance.

## APIs
- An API is the **gateway between frontend and backend**, giving access to functions and
  data within a platform *and* with partners — creating new business models.
- Partner examples from lecture: payment (Visa, Mastercard), logistics/shipping,
  **KYC-as-a-service** (e.g. Simple KYC) — subscribe rather than build; weeks of manual
  customer validation collapse into a subscription.
- **Build own strengths on core product, partner for standard functions** like payments and KYC.

## Aligning business and architecture (three-column slide)
1. **Architecture** — event-driven architecture (EDA) for real-time/high availability;
   modular not monolithic for growth; build core, partner for standard functions.
2. **Regulatory compliance by design** — compliance integrated at design phase with full
   auditability and explainability; RegTech automating regulatory reporting, transaction
   monitoring and risk assessment; adaptable to evolving and cross-border regulation.
3. **Communication and security architecture** — API-as-gatekeeper; cloud-native for ongoing
   security compliance; zero-trust architecture and privacy-enhancing technologies
   (federated learning, homomorphic encryption).

## Week 2 takeaway line to reuse
Aligning business objectives with architecture choice, adopting an **API-first approach**,
and practising **compliance and security by design** are the keys to a robust fintech platform.
