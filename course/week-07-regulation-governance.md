## The central claim
**Regulation and innovation are not a zero-sum game.** Regulation can be an *effective
strategy* for a fintech to stand out in the market. This inverts the usual student framing
of regulation as pure cost — and is exactly the kind of point a marker rewards.

## Regulatory landscape
Global regulators are converging on **risk-based frameworks, transparency and safety**,
despite different legal approaches across jurisdictions. Compare approaches across major
jurisdictions and identify the common trend rather than treating each as unique.

## Compliance mapped to lifecycles
Obligations map to both the **data lifecycle** and the **model lifecycle**.
AI-specific data lifecycle considerations:
- data **privacy by design**
- **training data vs operational data** distinction
- **prompt data**
- **output accuracy**

## Operational resilience
Be ready to demonstrate to regulators that your **incident response capability is credible
and well rehearsed**. Not "we have a policy" — rehearsed.

## Ethics and AI governance
Must balance **fairness, explainability, accountability and consumer protection** — and must
support regulatory evidence by explaining *what was done, how results were measured, and
how the response was made*. Governance is an evidence-production function.

## Regulator–innovator partnership
Regulators proactively engage innovators through **supervisory support, expert guidance and
sandboxes**, fast-tracking solution testing and risk mitigation. If your capstone faces a
regulatory barrier, a sandbox pathway is a credible mitigation — not a hand-wave.
