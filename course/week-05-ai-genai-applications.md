# Week 5 — Trending AI and GenAI Applications in Finance

## The profit-pool argument (strong capstone opener)
**Non-lending, fee-based businesses of conventional banks deliver significantly higher
profit margins than their lending business** — which is exactly why fintechs attack them
directly. If your solution targets fee income, say so and cite this.

## Underserved lending
Fintechs address previously underserved lending markets through: **BNPL, embedded lending,
peer-to-peer lending, alternative credit**.

## Where fintechs help banks
Fintechs play notable roles helping banks address major **risk categories** — position a
B2B-to-bank solution here rather than as a competitor.

## The two showcase applications
- **Alternative credit scoring** — non-traditional data assessing creditworthiness.
- **Hyper-personalisation** — data and AI/GenAI tailoring the offer.

Both demonstrate the same chain: data + AI → **financial inclusion**, better customer
experience and engagement → higher **economic and societal impact**. That chain is the
cleanest available justification for a socially-framed capstone.

## RegTech
The industry is accelerating adoption of modern RegTech, **combining rule-based, ML and
GenAI** for more robust monitoring and compliance reporting. Note the combination — pure
GenAI compliance is not the course's position.

## Deployment discipline
Successful AI/GenAI deployment requires: close examination of **risks and constraints**,
monitoring of **performance metrics**, and implementation of **guardrails**. Mirror these
three in the capstone risk section.

## Case: Kabbage (extended reading)
US small-business lender using AI and data analytics — real-time bank account, transaction
and accounting-platform data — for credit decisions in minutes, versus traditional banks'
demands on cash flow, credit score and collateral. Acquired by American Express; later
Chapter 11 as KServicing Wind Down Corp (Oct 2022). Customer service and PPP handling drew
criticism.
**Use it as a two-sided case**: automation unlocked an underserved segment *and* produced
service failures and regulatory exposure. A capstone that cites Kabbage only for the
upside is missing the point.
